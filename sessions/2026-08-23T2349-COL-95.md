## Two things I had wrong

**"The data-protection promise is the intersection of every vendor."** Falsified by the per-tenant residency answer. With per-tenant routing, a tenant requiring Australian processing is simply never routed to a vendor that cannot provide it — one weak vendor does not set the ceiling for everyone. Vendor-independence stops being a risk to manage and becomes the mechanism that makes per-client residency sellable. §10.1 reconciled with §10.2 so they no longer contradict.

**The WHA flow-down framing.** I built a paragraph around WHA carrying government flow-down obligations. **WHA does not use the product.** That came from the original audit brief and I carried it far past its evidence. Corrected in place.

Also corrected earlier the same day: **funding is ordered, not exclusive.** My exclusive `company`-XOR-`self` model failed the requirement outright — a user with a company allowance could not top up without surrendering it. The objection I had raised (precedence causes "why did it charge me" tickets) was about *ambiguity*, not *ordering*; a fixed, stated order is not ambiguous.

## The finding that reframes the market

**PSPF Policy Advisory 001-2025** confirms **OFFICIAL information may be used with generative AI**, conditional on the provider holding **Hosting Certification Framework** certification. Demand is unlocked, and HCF is the *named* gate — so it stops being a later nice-to-have and becomes the thing that opens the market. OFFICIAL is the realistic target; PROTECTED only if a deal demands it.

**Marked UNVERIFIED and left that way:** the source appeared to name specific vendors as HCF-certified, but the phrasing was ambiguous and came from secondary sources rather than the register. It decides whether the four-vendor set can serve OFFICIAL workloads at all. **A fresh session should check `hostingcertification.gov.au` directly and record the date.** The strategic position is provisional until settled.

## The rule most likely to be broken silently

Written into both the spec and the metering page as its own subsection:

> **`ai_usage` must never store question or answer text.**

The moment a prompt lands in the ledger, the ledger becomes client content and inherits the residency constraint — in a region that probably violates it. It will not look like a mistake when it happens: someone adds a `prompt` column to debug a bad answer, or logs the payload on error. Both are reasonable instincts. So the mitigations are structural — an allowlist of columns with no free-text field, error handlers that log a `request_id` and never a payload, and a CI check that fails if a content-shaped column appears.
